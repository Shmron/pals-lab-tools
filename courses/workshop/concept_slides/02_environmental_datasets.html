Concept: where does environmental data come from?

Most of sub-Saharan Africa has limited ground-sensor networks. So instead of weather stations, this field leans on satellite products and reanalysis (model + observation blends).

VariableTypical sourceTemporal resolutionNotes
TemperatureERA5-Land (ECMWF)Hourly → dailyReanalysis: model + observations
PM2.5 / air qualityCAMS (Copernicus)3-hourly → dailyGlobal atmospheric composition
NDVI (vegetation)MODIS16-day compositeNot daily in reality — a real caveat
PrecipitationCHIRPSDailySatellite infrared + gauge stations
Why it matters: this table is the single most reusable thing in the whole course — it's the lookup participants will reach for whenever they design their own exposure study.

Concept: the nearest-cell lookup

Environmental data lives on a grid (one value per cell). Health data lives at points (a facility, a village). To attach an environmental value to a point, find the nearest grid-cell centroid — fast, even across millions of points, using a k-d tree.

Grid centroids: × × × × × × × × × × × × × × Your point: ★ × × × × × k-d tree query(★) → nearest × → that cell's grid_id → merge its temp/PM2.5/NDVI onto your point
Why a k-d tree, not a brute-force loop: it turns an O(n×m) nearest-neighbour search into roughly O(n log m) — the difference between seconds and hours once you scale to thousands of points and millions of grid-cell-days (which is exactly what Notebook 04 needs).
