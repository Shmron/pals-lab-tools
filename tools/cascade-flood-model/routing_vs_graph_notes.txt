ROUTING vs GRAPH APPROACH — OSRM (PRECISE) vs NetworkX+pyrosm (CASCADE flood pipeline)
=========================================================================

OSRM (PRECISE's approach) — external routing engine, Docker servers per country/mode.
Production-grade, fast at query time (compiled, contraction hierarchies), industry-standard.
Two real problems for our use case:

1. Custom speed logic gets harder.
   Our "31% no vehicle access" fix — treating informal/untagged roads as slow-but-driveable
   rather than excluded, surface-based speed factors — was built by writing our own edge
   weights in NetworkX precisely because we needed that control. OSRM would need a custom
   Lua speed profile to replicate it. Doable, but real extra work, not a given.

2. Flood-edge removal doesn't fit OSRM's model well.
   OSRM bakes edge weights in during preprocessing (osrm-extract / partition / customize).
   To simulate "these 118 crossings just failed," you'd either re-run that whole
   preprocessing pipeline per scenario (slow, disk-heavy) or use OSRM's live traffic-update
   mechanism to down-weight specific ways (more advanced, avoids a full rebuild, but still
   extra machinery we don't currently need).

NetworkX + pyrosm (our approach) — everything in-memory, no server.
G.remove_edges_from() for a flood scenario is instant. Full control over edge weights —
exactly what let us build the informal-road model, facility closure, and the
flooded/baseline graph pair with zero extra infrastructure.
Tradeoff: slower per-query (Dijkstra from scratch), and the real bottleneck right now is
actually pyrosm parsing the national PBF and building the graph each run (minutes) — that's
independent of Dijkstra vs OSRM either way.

RECOMMENDATION
--------------
Don't force one universal choice — these two engines are good at different jobs.

- PRECISE's OSRM stays for the NORMAL scenario (no edge removal needed — exactly OSRM's
  sweet spot: fast, accurate, "what's the travel time right now").
- Our NetworkX graph stays for the DISTURBED scenario (scenario perturbation — removing
  specific crossings — is the actual point, and our current runtime, a few minutes per
  site, is fine for a run-once-per-event workflow, not a live interactive tool).

Reconsider OSRM for the flood side only if:
  (a) this needs to become interactive/instant (e.g. a live dashboard), or
  (b) it scales to national-level networks where per-query Dijkstra genuinely becomes
      a bottleneck.

If the two pipelines merge later, the natural integration isn't "pick one router" — it's
PRECISE's OSRM output feeding the baseline numbers (consistency with existing PRECISE
study data), and our NetworkX graph handling the flooded-scenario delta on top of it.
